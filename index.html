About

I am a PhD candidate in Statistics at the University of Cincinnati, working with Professor Xia Wang, and I expect to graduate in December 2026. My research focuses on developing scalable and robust Bayesian methods for variable selection in high-dimensional and incomplete data, with applications to biomedical data.

Alongside my dissertation, I also work as a statistician at Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center, where I analyze proteomic and clinical data to identify biomarkers associated with disease. Previously, I worked as a statistical programmer at Procter & Gamble digital accelerator center, developing statistical tools and analytical applications.

Research

My dissertation, Scalable and Robust Bayesian Methods for Variable Selection in High-Dimensional and Incomplete Data, focuses on developing Bayesian methods for variable selection when p ≫ n and the data are incomplete. My work includes continuous and discrete spike-and-slab models, Pólya-Gamma data augmentation, and scalable computational methods, with applications to biomedical and proteomic data.

I am also very interested in machine learning and its intersection with statistical methodology, particularly for analyzing complex, high-dimensional data. I am interested in developing and applying methods that combine statistical rigor with modern machine learning approaches to address real-world problems.
